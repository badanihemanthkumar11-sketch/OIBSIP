01

A Curious Mind

Albert Einstein was a German-born physicist whose ideas transformed modern science and reshaped humanity's understanding of the universe.

Born in Ulm, Germany, in 1879, Einstein showed an early fascination with the world around him. Rather than simply accepting familiar explanations, he was drawn to questions about light, motion, space, and time. His independent nature and willingness to challenge conventional thinking became important characteristics throughout his life.

Einstein's scientific career changed dramatically in 1905, a year later remembered as his "annus mirabilis," or miraculous year. During this period he published several influential papers covering topics such as Brownian motion, the photoelectric effect, and special relativity. These works helped establish him as a major scientific thinker.

His theory of general relativity, published in 1915, provided a new description of gravity. Instead of treating gravity simply as a force between objects, the theory connected gravity with the curvature of spacetime. Its predictions made Einstein internationally famous and became an important foundation of modern physics.

Beyond his scientific achievements, Einstein was known for his humanitarian interests and his belief in independent thought. He spoke publicly about peace, civil rights, education, and the dangers of nationalism and war. His legacy therefore extends beyond equations: it also represents curiosity, imagination, intellectual courage, and the importance of questioning assumptions.
